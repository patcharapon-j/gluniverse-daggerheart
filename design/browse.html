THE WINDOWKinds, then that kind's own filters, then the thing itself

Foundry's compendium sidebar gives you a pack and a flat alphabetical list of names. Every fact anybody filters on — domain, level, Recall Cost, tier, trait, range, burden — lives in system, and the sidebar cannot see any of it, so "a Grace card at level 3 or lower I can afford to recall" means scrolling 189 names and opening them one at a time. This system already knows those, because they are config.ts's closed sets. The rail asks what kind of thing first, and everything under it belongs to that kind: a filter rail that was the union of every subtype's axes would be nine-tenths greyed out over whatever was on screen. Each chip carries the count it would leave behind, and a chip with none is dead rather than hidden — a filter set that drops its empty values looks complete while lying about the shape of the collection.

THE CARD GRIDA printed card is drawn as one, and nothing peeks

This is the creation window's argument arriving with nothing to choose. A class, a domain card, an ancestry and a subclass are printed objects, and a text summary of one lies by omission about the two facts a card carries structurally rather than in prose — the domain, which is the hue and the corner sigils, and the level and Recall Cost, which are the corner blocks. So they are drawn as cards, at 196px rather than make.css's 176: there the grid is a chooser and more options at once is worth a smaller card, here the card is the answer and the rules text printed on it is what you came to read. Nothing hover-peeks, for the reason the creation window renders no peek layer at all — the card is already on screen at card size, and a hover card over a card is the same picture twice. A click opens the document's own sheet.

A PAGE AT A TIMEThe grid is paged and the table is not

A .card is a container-query root that measures its own wrapped prose: fit() writes a type scale, reads scrollHeight and steps until the panel stops overflowing, and every step is a forced synchronous layout. A hundred and eighty-nine of them on one document is a hundred and eighty-nine container roots and several hundred reflows, and it is what took Foundry down the first time this window was opened in it. A table row is a grid row with text in it and costs none of that, and the two readings differ the same way — nobody compares a hundred and eighty-nine paintings at once, while running an eye down three hundred and fifty-eight rows of the same five columns is exactly what a table is for. So this control, and only under the grid. It is its own sentinel: scrolling to it and pressing it are the same act, which is swap.js's rule about a surface that is both dragged and pressed. Nothing is dropped silently — the count above the grid says how many matched and this says how many of them are drawn.

THE TABLETwo hundred longswords are five columns of the same five facts

Equipment is not an object you hold a picture of. A longsword is one line of chapter 2, and what you compare across two hundred of them is trait, range, damage, burden and feature — the same five facts, which a tile states in five different places on every tile and a column is read down. subgrid is what lines the columns up across groups, so two tables of the same facts cannot solve their widths differently. The head is sticky here and is not in the creation window, and that is a difference in length rather than in taste: thirty-five rows inside a step against three hundred and fifty-eight here, and a column heading you have scrolled past is a column heading that has stopped working. There is no artwork — the equipment packs have none, and a picture on nine rows out of three hundred and fifty-eight reads as broken rather than as sparse — so the one picture is the design's own type glyph at row height.
