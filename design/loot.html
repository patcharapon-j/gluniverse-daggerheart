// DECIDEDB, the X, and the word — with one change

Q1 · B, but as rows rather than tiles. Loot with a rules paragraph earns a card; a torch stays a line. The card is not laid out in the panel though — the panel is the same spine the domain loadout uses, with the full card on hover and pinned on click. A hover peek dies the moment you move toward it, which is fine for “which card is this” and useless for “read this card”, and a rules paragraph is something you read. Click again, click anywhere else, or press Escape to drop it.
Q2 · the crossed box, and it crosses from the left like every other row of boxes on the sheet. The neutral mark now names its own colour — see below.
Q3 · a word. No ramp. The rarity is in the footer and the footer is enough.

The X had a real bug in it, and it was invisible in exactly the wrong direction. The three tracks each name their own --mk; a mark drawn outside all three — an advancement slot, a consumable charge — named none, and fill:var(--mk) with nothing to resolve is an invalid property that falls back to SVG's initial fill, which is black. On a dark sheet that is an X you cannot see: the box reads as unmarked. The advancement slots had been papering over it with their own fill:var(--ink), which is why it never showed up. The neutral mark names --ink itself now, in one place, and the slots' copy is gone.

// WHAT THE RULES SAYThree things, and one of them is already done

Treasure is finished. There is no treasure object in Daggerheart — loot is items and consumables, and money is gold, which is handfuls, bags and chests and is already on the rail.

Items are loot you keep and use repeatedly until you lose them. Consumables are used once, and the rule that matters is this one: you can hold up to five of each consumable at a time. That makes a consumable the first thing on this sheet that is both a card and a resource — a count that goes down — and this sheet already owns two grammars for that. Both kinds carry the same four rarities: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary.

// QUESTION 1What earns a card

Your inventory currently reads “Torch, 50 ft of rope, a rival’s letter”. A torch is not a card. But a Gem of Alacrity’s text is a rule, and a rule you cannot see is a rule you will not use. Same loot in all three columns.

// QUESTION 2How five charges go to zero

Same consumable, four counters, all in the same place — a row of its own under the name, so the only thing being compared is the counter. The question underneath is which grammar a consumable belongs to: quantity held, which on this sheet is the gold purse’s filled pips, or quantity spent, which is the crossed box. They are opposites, and picking the wrong one means the row reads backwards under pressure.

// QUESTION 3How rarity reads

Four steps, and this is the same wall we hit with tier: there is no hue left to spend. The difference is that a party’s loot genuinely spans all four rarities at once, where their weapons are all the same tier — so the value ramp that bought nothing there may buy something here.
