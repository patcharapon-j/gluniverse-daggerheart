// THE d6Advantage is one die, with a sign

Advantage adds a d6; disadvantage subtracts one. They cancel one-for-one across every source — the book is explicit that you will never roll both — so this is one object carrying a sign rather than two independent slots. A d6's face is a square, so that is the silhouette, and it is drawn smaller than the duality dice because it is a smaller die and because the pair should stay the subject of the strip.

Advantage is a lit steel chip. Disadvantage is the same chip as a negative — dark face, lit rim. Same die doing the opposite job, and no new hue spent on something the arithmetic line already names in words. The sign lives on the arithmetic, where it can be read.

More than one d6 happens exactly one way: Help an Ally. Any number of PCs can spend a Hope to help, each rolls a d6, and you take the highest. So several land and one counts — and the ones that don't are crossed off with an X.

Unlit alone was not enough, and the reason is worth stating: the losing duality die is unlit too, and that die still counts — both duality dice are summed into the total. A helper's d6 that didn't win is thrown away entirely. Two different fates were wearing the same face. So the X means one thing and one thing only — this did not count — nothing else on any card is allowed to use it, and it's drawn in --xmark-p, the material this system already uses for a struck-out scar. It does largely cover the numeral, and that is the trade rather than a compromise: a discarded helper’s face value is trivia, the arithmetic line already reports the one that counted, and a strike you have to look twice at is not a strike.

Each die also tumbles inside its own range on arrival: a d6 that flashes an 11 on its way down is a d6 lying about what it is.

// REACTION ROLLSThe duality is rolled, then thrown away

A reaction roll uses the Duality Dice and then discards the duality: no Hope, no Fear, no GM move, and nobody may help you. So it does not get a Hope or Fear field — colouring it gold would promise a token nobody is going to hand you. Graphite, and the dice keep their own colours, because the dice are still the dice.

It has nothing to claim either, so the action row carries the consequence instead — which is what a reaction roll is actually for. And with no Difficulty there is nothing true it can say, so it says nothing: the sentence is omitted and the total stands alone. No placeholder, no chip, no announcement that a Difficulty is missing. A card narrating its own ignorance is worse than a card that is quiet — the dice, the total and the arithmetic are all still there and all still correct. Same rule on every card type: a missing Difficulty is not a fact worth a slot.

A critical reaction is still the top rung and still red — but it isn't Hope. You clear no Stress and gain no Hope; you ignore the effects that would still have hit you on a success.

// DAMAGEA quantity, not an outcome

No duality axis and no verdict — the only question about a damage roll is how big. So the parts take new jobs: the sentence slot carries the damage type, which is the one thing about a damage number that changes what happens to it; the meta line carries the notation; the number is the card. Proficiency is the die count, so a Proficiency of 3 with a d8 weapon is three chips, and a critical at Proficiency 6 is twelve.

And the card stops at the number. How many Hit Points this becomes depends on thresholds, armour, resistance and immunity that all live on the target — so it states the damage and offers to apply it, rather than claiming an outcome it cannot know. Same posture the duality card takes toward the fiction.

Critical damage starts from the highest value the dice could roll, then rolls them as usual and adds. That is two different kinds of number in one row, so the struck set is tagged MAX, drawn as the deep cut with a bright rim, and — the part that matters — it does not tumble on arrival. It was not rolled, it was awarded, and animating it would be the one visual claim on this card that is false.

And it is the black bar, not the wound plate. Endfield's other and more-used register: near-black, with the colour reduced to a lit lower edge and the numeral. It draws a line the family needed — colour means something happened to you; damage is a number you hand to someone else — and it keeps the only two saturated reds in the system from landing one message apart.
