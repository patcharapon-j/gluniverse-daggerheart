// THE d20The one other die that earns a silhouette

The rule on the player side was only the duality die gets a shape — a d8 face and a d10 face are shapes nobody recognises, and at 26px none of them read apart. The d20 clears that bar anyway, for a reason that has nothing to do with recognising polygons: in this game a d20 is rolled exactly once — by the GM, at you. It never competes with another shape for meaning, so the silhouette isn't asking you to tell two things apart. It's telling you, from across a scrolling log, which side of the table the card came from.

Like the d12 it is drawn as the solid, not one face of it — and unlike the d12 it needs no stylisation to get there. Viewed down a 3-fold axis an icosahedron's silhouette is a regular hexagon and its front face a triangle at 0.618 of the radius, φ−1, the same golden section the solid is built from. So the shape on the card is the true projection, and it reads as a d20 on sight.

Advantage on this side is a second d20 taken high; disadvantage a second taken low. Not the PC's added d6 — a different mechanic entirely. But the grammar is the one you already learned: several land, one counts, and the ones that didn't are crossed off with the X. That's the return on having given the X exactly one meaning.

A natural 20 hits regardless and starts damage at maximum, so it takes the top rung's red. It was still rolled, though — unlike the awarded damage dice, it tumbles on arrival.

// THE GM CARDThe GM is a force, not a participant

Near-black throughout rather than a field on a card, one lit rail down the left edge, the type pushed to mono, the number two sizes down so the card reports rather than announces. The part carrying most of the work is invisible until you look for it: no cut corner. That notch is the family mark on every player card in this system, so its absence says not one of yours before any hue does.

The rail carries the outcome. The card has exactly one mark, so the mark does the work — red on a hit, cold steel on a miss, hot on a critical. Before that the two outcomes were told apart only by the words, which is the slowest possible way to answer the fastest question a GM card is ever asked: did it land.

And the number is ink, not red. It was the wound highlight — which is the damage bar's numeral colour, and a damage card lands one message after this one. Two different objects arriving back to back in the same clothes took back the one thing this treatment was bought to do. Red means a quantity of harm now and nothing else; a d20 against Evasion is a comparison, and it is set in ink.

The rules underneath are the confirmed ones: d20 + the stat block's Attack Modifier against the target's Evasion, meet-or-beat hits; a Fear may be spent to add an Experience (marked violet — the currency that bought it, the way a PC's spent Hope is gold); a natural 20 auto-hits and criticals; and a critical on an adversary reaction roll has no added benefit, so it gets no material either.

// THE ONE THAT LOSTSame plate, hostile field

Kept as the record, the way B–E are on the plate page. It was the player's plate one field further along — everything you use to parse a card left where it was, only the die, the field and the subject of the sentence changing. It read cleanly in isolation, and that is exactly the trap: in the log the GM came out looking like one more participant at the table, wearing a portrait and a coloured field like everybody else.

// IN A LOGWhere it actually got decided

A card is never judged alone, and the log is what settled this. One exchange — the PC attacks, deals damage, the adversary answers, the PC reacts — with both treatments in place. The question was never which card is prettier; it was whether you can tell whose turn it is without reading a word, and whether the cost of doing so is paid on every scroll.

The log also turned up the one thing neither card showed in isolation: the othered card collided with the damage bar. Near-black body, colour down to one lit edge, a red numeral — that register was already taken, by the card immediately above it. Two different objects arriving one message apart in the same clothes took back the very thing othering was bought to do. That is why the numeral is ink now and the rail is the only red on the card, and it is the sort of fault only adjacency exposes.
