// DECIDEDB — warm it. #8f8578 / #6b6357

Shipped in domains.js. Bone keeps its value and changes temperature: old ivory rather than neutral grey. It is the smallest possible departure from the printed colour — a player who has seen a physical Bone card still recognises the row — and it is the only move that puts Bone in a family of its own, because warm is the one direction nothing else on this sheet occupies.

One pair, both themes. A darker light-theme variant reads better on white paper, and the study carried one for a while, but no other domain has a per-theme value and Bone does not earn the mechanism: --dom is decorative here — every white-on-colour surface already draws from --dom-dk — and a domain whose identity colour shifts with the sheet chrome is a domain a player cannot learn. Option A is still rendered below in its printed colour, so the two are comparable.

// THE MEASUREMENTIt is not a contrast problem

Bone reads 4.79 against the dark sheet and 3.42 against the light one — better than five of the other eight domains on the dark side. Contrast is not what is wrong with it. What is wrong is one number: saturation 0. Every other domain sits between 50 and 87 — and the non-domain graphite sits at 16, which is where the real trouble is. Bone and “no domain” are the only two entries in this whole palette from the same family, and they are told apart by value alone. Every other domain is separated from graphite by hue, which nothing erodes; value is exactly what a colour ramp laid over artwork does erode.

// THE COLLISIONGrey already means something here

This system spent grey early and deliberately: a saturated hue means domain, and graphite means no domain. That is why Ancestry, Community and every piece of equipment are #5c636d with the colour ramp suppressed — a sword belongs to no domain, so it gets no hue. Bone is a domain wearing the one colour reserved for things that are not domains. Below: a Bone card and three non-domain cards, as they actually appear.
