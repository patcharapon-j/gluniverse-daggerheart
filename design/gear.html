// THE QUESTIONWeapons and armour have no domain — should they have a hue?

Every card in this system says which domain it belongs to with a saturated colour, and the nine domains have taken most of the wheel: purple, red, grey, blue, magenta, teal, green, olive, orange. Gold is Hope and violet is Fear. Ancestry and Community are graphite because a heritage has no domain, and equipment currently shares that graphite for the same reason.

That works right up until the gear tab, where four graphite tiles sit in a row and nothing distinguishes a bow from a breastplate at a glance. The question is whether that is a problem worth spending a colour on — and there is no colour left to spend, so anything below that introduces one is really introducing an ambiguity: you would see a coloured chip and have to ask which domain it was before realising it was not one.

// THE SLOT GLYPHSThree marks, told apart by count

The slot is the one thing about a piece of gear you cannot read off its own stats, so it gets the mark rather than the word. All three are drawn the way the ancestry and community glyphs are — filled paths, no strokes, chamfers rather than curves — and they are discriminated by count, because that is what survives 13px, which is the size the tile's chip actually renders at. A primary weapon is one spearhead: a point is the only weapon idea that is not a genre, where a crossguard is immediately fantasy. A secondary is two of the same head at 56%, which is also the literal reading of the slot. Armor is three bands tapering downward — no points anywhere, which is the whole distinction, and tapering because a stack that narrows is a torso while a stack that does not is a menu icon. Judge them at 13px; the rest of the strip is only there to show what is being lost.

// THE ARGUMENTWhat the chip already has room for

The chip on a domain card holds a numeral above a mark, and the numeral is the card's level — the one number you sort and shop by. A weapon has exactly one number like that and it is its tier. Option B puts the tier where the level goes, using the component that already exists, in the position your eye already checks. It costs no hue, it cannot be confused with a domain, and it survives the light theme, a screenshot and a colour-blind reader without any of them being a special case — which is the same argument that made the three damage marks differ by substance before they differed by colour.
