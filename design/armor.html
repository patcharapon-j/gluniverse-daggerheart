// A · THE BARA number line, not a stat block

A threshold is a boundary, not a property of either side of it — so the numerals sit astride the joins on their own plates rather than inside the cells, and each join is a hard vertical gold rule. A diagonal join would have been more Endfield and would have blurred the one thing this component exists to state precisely. The bounded zones are drawn to scale against each other: chainmail at 7/15 has spans of 7 and 8, so its Major band is visibly wider than its Minor — that is the whole reason this is a bar and not four numbers in boxes. The last zone has no upper bound, so it gets a fixed width and an arrow rather than a lie about where it ends. Severity escalates by weight — from the paper's own value away to its opposite — because hue in this system means domain and damage belongs to none. Which means the ramp has to reverse with the theme: on white paper Severe is the near-black zone, on a dark sheet it is the near-white one. It is the only colour in the system that inverts against the theme rather than with it.

// B · IN CONTEXTThe bar is a sheet component

The bar earns its space on the sheet, where the thresholds are a thing you read under pressure every time you take a hit. On the card and the tile it does not: those already have a data strip, and the bar had to shove aside the description to fit. So they get one cell — Thresholds 9 / 20 — which is how the rules and every armour table quote them anyway. Splitting Major and Severe across two cells was inventing a distinction the game does not make.
