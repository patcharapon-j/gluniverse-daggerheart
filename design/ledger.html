The change log

Everything else this system posts is an event somebody chose: a roll, a card shown, a rest taken. This is the opposite — the record of what happened to a sheet while nobody was posting anything. A player marks three Stress and the GM, looking at the map, has no idea; a card comes out of the vault and the only witness is the sheet it happened on. The rules ask a table to keep these numbers where everyone can see them, and until now the only place any of them existed was the sheet of the person holding it.

// THE UNITNet, not keystrokes

The unit is the change, not the write. Applying damage is four writes — armour, stress, hope, hit points — and one event. Fixing a miscount is three writes in two seconds and one correction. So entries coalesce and what lands is the net, never +1, +1, −1, +2. Which also means a net of zero posts nothing, and taking back a box you marked by mistake is not something the table has to watch happen.

one hit four writes, one card

This is a Severe hit with an Armor Slot spent. Four separate writes to the document, inside about fifty milliseconds, and one thing that happened. The card is the event, not the transaction log.

a short rest's worth three changes, one card

A run of dim marks ending in ghosts is a heal. Ending in bright ones it is a hit. That is the whole reading, and there is nothing to read: no numeral on this card says it, because the strip already has.

// THE SIZEA note in the margin, not an object in the log

A plate is 300px of card because a roll is the thing everybody is looking at. This arrives unasked for, while somebody is describing a room — and four of them between two rolls must not push the roll off the screen. So a change is one line. The card that stood about 230px tall for three changes stands about 60, and the thing that bought most of it was the numerals: a 22px figure and the word MARKED, sitting above a strip that had already said both.

four changes four lines

Every row is a label and a picture. The label has a floor rather than a width, so the tracks line their strips up — that is what makes two of them comparable at a glance — while a card's own name, which is as long as it is, pushes its counters right instead of ellipsing to nothing.

one box size per message, not per row

Twelve Hit Points over six Stress. Sized independently that is two different boxes in one glance, which reads as two different kinds of thing. They are not — they are the same box, and the shorter row simply ends early. MARKS made this argument first.

// THE MARKThe sheet's arm, without the sheet's svg

Crossing a box off has a look in this system and it is not a filled square: a wound is a tear that bows off the diagonal, a strain is a fine scored line, a plate is a chisel blow. That is mark.js's whole argument — the tracks are told apart by substance first and hue second — and a log drawing three identical squares in three colours would throw away the half of it that survives being small. So these are the same arms, off the same point lists, through armPolygon: three of the four are pure point lists, so a clip-path is exactly the same shape rather than an approximation of one.

the same three arms sheet left, log right

One geometry, two means of drawing it. The sheet's is an <svg> with a highlight down the arm, a second scratch on the strain and three cracks running out of the chisel. The log's is the arm alone, cut out of an empty element — everything below about a pixel is gone at this size anyway, and what is left is the profile, which is the part that identifies the track.

the four states on, up, dn, bare

.on is context and is deliberately quiet. .up is what this entry marked, at full strength and lit. .dn is what it gave back, drawn as the ghost of the mark that was there — a cleared box on the sheet is simply empty and is right to be, because the sheet says what is true now; a log has to say what changed, and “there was a mark here” is a thing only the mark can say.

// THE OTHER SHAPESHope is gems, a card's pool is chits

Neither is redrawn here. Hope is gems everywhere in this system and a card's counters are the row the loadout draws — the log is not the place to introduce a second drawing of either. What changes is that nothing is pressable, and that both learn the strip's two change states: a gained pip is lit and glowing, a spent one is the socket it left with the shape of what was there drawn back over it.

hope and a scarred slot

A scarred slot is not an empty one. The ceiling is four and the row is six wide — one lit, three spent, two dead. Drawing four would quietly drop the permanent half of what happened to this character; drawing six lit sockets would promise Hope she cannot hold.

a card's counters and a card crossing

A move has no quantity, so it takes no strip. A name and a direction: the arrow is drawn and the destination is named, which is one fact each rather than the same fact twice. The pip is the domain — the one hue on this card that is not a track's material, and right here for the reason it is right everywhere: the colour is how you know which card.

// STORAGEWhy none of this is an svg

Foundry's sanitiser strips every <svg> out of stored message content. A posted card survives that by being redrawn from a flag on render. A mark track cannot: a marked box that loses its X does not look broken, it looks unmarked, which is a lie rather than a degradation. So this is not the sheet's MARKS row — every mark here is a hole cut in an empty element, GEMS and CHITS were already <i> and <b>, and the whole message survives storage exactly as posted. No flag, no redraw, no render hook.

the same card, unstyled what a foreign client sees

Truthful, and complete. Stylesheet off, markup intact — and the numerals the card stopped drawing are still in it, one clipped sentence per row. A picture reaches two readers badly: somebody using a screen reader, and a client that never got this file. Both get the sentence, which is the same element assistive technology needed anyway.
