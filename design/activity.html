The activity log

The change log went to chat for as long as it existed, and chat is the wrong room for it. Everything else this system posts is an event somebody chose — a roll, a card shown, a rest taken — and the ledger's whole argument is that it is the opposite: the record of what happened while nobody was posting anything. A four-person party in a fight settles a dozen tracks a round, and every one of those cards pushed the roll everybody was looking at further up a log four people are reading. The only relief available was switching it off, which took the record away from the one person it was for. So it moves to the GM, who is who the rules ask to keep it, and it becomes a window: there when you look at it, silent when you do not.

// THE WINDOWA head that does not scroll, and a body that does

Two regions. The head holds the count and the two presses that are about the log rather than about anything in it, and it sits outside the scroller because a count you have to scroll to find is not a count. The body is the scroller, and it runs newest first — the opposite of chat, and right here: the question this window answers is always what just happened, so the answer goes where you are already looking and nothing ever has to be auto-scrolled underneath the scrollbar you are holding.

An empty log is ambiguous in a way an empty list is not: it means either that nothing has happened or that nothing is being watched. This one is the first.

The label on the press changes and a banner appears, because the two states of an empty window look identical otherwise. The banner takes height rather than overlaying — edit mode's rule, and the same reasoning: it lasts until somebody changes it back.

A panel rebuilt at its new length has already arrived, so there would be nothing left to animate — and the twenty cards underneath would replay every time anything landed. Press the button.
