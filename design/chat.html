// THE PROBLEMTwo axes, and the sheet only knows one of them

Every action roll resolves on two axes at once, and they are independent. Hope or Fear is decided by the dice against each other — always knowable, needs nothing from anyone, and it is the axis that changes table state: you gain a Hope, or the GM does. Success or failure is decided by the total against a Difficulty — which the rules explicitly let the GM keep to themselves, and which does not exist at all until a target is picked.

So the card can always say “15, with Fear” and can only sometimes say “and that missed”. Which is the whole design: this card is authoritative about arithmetic and tentative about fiction. It states what the dice did, it states what is owed, and it says vs ? rather than guessing at a verdict it was never given. The only place the two axes touch is the critical — equal faces succeed regardless of the total.

// THE OPTIONSHow much authority does the card have?

Same roll, three answers. The dice are identical in all three columns; what differs is how much the card is willing to conclude and how much it does on your behalf.

// EVERY STATEFive outcomes, three kinds, and the awkward ones

The five action-roll results, plus the three cases that are not action rolls at all: a reaction roll, which generates neither Hope nor Fear and so gets neither colour; a damage roll, which has no axes; and a critical damage roll, which starts from the maximum the dice could roll and then rolls them anyway — the rule most often got wrong at the table, and the best argument for the card showing its arithmetic.
