// THE OBJECTA counter that says something

A chit answers how many. Eighteen rules in the corpus ask a second question it has no room for: how many, and what does each one say. Prayer Dice are four d4s lying on your sheet with four different faces up, and four identical counters are not a record of that. So the tray holds dice — drawn with die.js, the same builder the chat plate uses, so the two can never disagree about what a d10 looks like.

It is a chits row wearing a second class. Sockets, the way to put one down, the gap, the tilt, the six host sizings and both grounds are already written and already right, and a die is a counter that says something — it belongs in the same tray, on the same corner of the same plate. What keep.css adds is only the difference.

// THE BLANKAn unrolled die is a chit

Slayer Dice and the Sigil's d8s are placed and rolled later; Prayer Dice are rolled when placed. So a die here may have no face — and one with no face draws the family rhombus where its numeral goes, which is exactly what a chit draws and for the same reason: it is what stops the object reading as a blank at 14px. A pool you have not rolled is a row of counters, and it becomes dice at the moment that means something.

// THE INKA numeral that survives nine domains

Every other die in this system is painted by hand — Hope is gold with dark ink, Fear is violet with white. A kept die takes the card's hue, and the nine domains run from Splendor's pale gold to Midnight's near-black, so one ink colour cannot be right. The face's own lightness is the whole question, so the numeral asks it: clamp(0,(l - .62) * -100,1) is black above the threshold and white below it, with nothing in between and no third colour to decode. The blank's rhombus takes the same answer, because it stands in the same place and has the same problem.

Same anchor, same three corrections — the plate's own bottom edge, .lower's margin resolving against width, and the seam's 8cqw. Solved once in chit.css and inherited, which is the whole argument for the tray being a chits row.

// IN A ROWWhere a die needs more room than a counter

A chit at 14px shows a pip, which is a shape. A die at 14px shows a numeral at --nf of that — 8.5px on a d10 — and eight and a half pixels of Google Sans is the failure tile.css shipped when a 368px study panel met a 262px Foundry pane. So the row hosts take the tray to 17px and buy it back out of the gap, which they can afford because a die pool is at most five where a charge track is twelve.

// THE MOTIONPlacing, spending, rolling, climbing

The first two are chit.css's keyframes unchanged, and deliberately so: a die arrives on the card the way a counter does, because it arrives by the same gesture. The other two are this component's own. A tumble is the sheet saying it does not know the answer yet, and it has to end with a settle or the number reads as having been there all along. A tick is one number replacing another on an object that has not moved, so the die nudges rather than tumbles.

The step refuses at the top rather than wrapping or clamping, and that refusal is the card's whole bargain: when the die's value would exceed its maximum, remove the die. What the sheet must not do is decide for you — Wild Surge charges a Stress on the way out and Unstoppable does not, so the row says no and the rule is read by the person who wrote it.
