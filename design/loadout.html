// Q6 · THE LOADOUT ON THE SHEETThree densities, one panel width

A Daggerheart loadout is five active cards over a vault of the rest. The panel that has to hold them on a sheet is ~368px wide — drag the slider to test other widths, everything here is container-query driven and holds its proportions. The question is not which looks best in isolation, it is which of these you can use: find a card, read its cost, and know what it does, without opening anything. All three are the same component vocabulary — level cap, sigil plate, gold hairline, recall chip, tier bar — so whichever wins, a row still reads as the card it opens.

// C′ · THE SPINE IN USEHover to open the card

The spine only works if the card is one gesture away, so here is the gesture. What opens is the real card — the same component, the same one that goes to chat, at the size it was designed for. Nothing is redrawn for the preview, which is the point of building the sheet family out of the card's own parts. The level chip is now min-content wide: it holds a numeral and a mark, and every pixel beyond what those two need was a pixel taken off the name.

// D · THE CARD WITH ITS BODY DELETEDMinis

Same portrait, same corners, same seam, same badge — the description is simply gone, and the art takes the space it held. Its proportions are not the card's: with no prose to leave room for, the numeral, sigil and name all grow against the width, or a mini at a third of the size is just a card you cannot read. Three across is the whole point — two across is taller than the tiles for less information. The readout under each panel is the rendered size of the name and the type, because that is the trade: the spine compresses layout and keeps text at a fixed useful size, the mini compresses everything.

// B · WHAT THE TILE IS ALSO FORWeapons, armour, items

The tile is not only a loadout option — it is the shape you already asked for for equipment, which is why its data strip is generic. Tier and spellcast on a subclass, trait / range / damage / burden on a weapon: same component, same three cells, no new geometry. Graphite where there is no domain, exactly as on the cards.

It does not survive the trip to the sheet unchanged, and that is this page's own finding turned back on itself. The tile is judged here at 520px and holds up from about 368px. A Foundry pane hands it a 262px grid column — 289px at a default window — and because every measure in tile.css is cqw, everything shrinks by the same 0.79. A name going 18px → 14px is still a name. The stat labels go 6.3px → 4.9px and their values 9.1px → 7.2px, and those two rows are the only part of a weapon anyone reads twice. So the gear tab came out a column of large pictures with the information set as a caption underneath: too big, and saying too little. That is not a bug in any one number — it is what happens when a headline and a caption are scaled by the same factor, and it is the one place the container-query architecture does not simply pay for itself.

The fix is not px type, which would cost the property that makes this component work at all. It is to re-weight the tile for the width a sheet gives it: the art plate drops 36% → 25%, the labels come back to 6.1px and the values to 8.8px, the name steps down because it is no longer the only legible thing on the tile, the paragraph goes from three lines to two larger ones, and the box loses a tenth of its height. It lives in sheet.css under .slot .tile, .eqp .tile and .abl .sub .tile rather than in tile.css, because it is not a different tile — it is this sheet's column being narrow, which is a fact about the sheet. The base component is left exactly as drawn here for everything else that borrows one.

Bigger than that was measured and refused. At 3.4cqw the value reaches 9.8px and three of the four cells ellipse on a weapon the character actually carries, because four cells with a rule between each is the widest row this component ever holds and 203px is what a sheet column has. The longest row the game can print — KNOWLEDGE, VERY CLOSE, d12+9, TWO-HANDED — still truncates its last cell, and it truncated at the original size too: 196px of content in 169px of panel then, 226 in 203 now. That is what text-overflow:ellipsis has been on .stats .v for since it was written.
